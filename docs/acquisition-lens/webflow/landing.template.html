A focused job

Turn an interesting opportunity
into a useful next conversation.

A teaser can make a business look attractive long before the important facts are clear. Acquisition Lens helps a buyer organize that first review around fit, reported economics, and what will transfer after the owner leaves.

01 · Frame the opportunity

Why would you want to own it?

State the acquisition rationale and check the opportunity against your mandate.

02 · Separate fact from assertion

What supports the numbers?

Record reported revenue, earnings, asking price, and their source. Missing numbers remain unknown.

03 · Test transferability

What might change after closing?

Flag earnings adjustments, customer exposure, owner dependence, and unresolved transaction consents.

04 · Choose the next step

What must you verify next?

Get a preliminary disposition, an evidence-gap list, and questions for the next conversation.

The deliverable

A first-look brief.
A clearer diligence agenda.

The output brings the buyer’s rationale, reported economics, supporting inputs, unresolved risks, and next questions into one review. Download it as text or use the print layout to save a PDF.

“What would have to be true for us to keep pursuing this?”

The screen can recommend gathering basic facts, investigating open issues, advancing to initial diligence, setting an opportunity aside for the stated mandate, or using a specialist review.

Evidence before conviction.

Transparent decision rules

The screen explains which answers generated a concern or a follow-up question. Unknowns remain visible. A single score does not compress away the issues.

A defined stopping point

A first look helps allocate diligence time. Valuation, normalized earnings, financing, downside modeling, and an investment committee decision belong in the next stage.

For buyers reviewing established operating businesses. Startups, distressed situations, minority investments, and businesses without positive reported earnings need a different analysis. All conclusions rely on the buyer’s inputs; no underlying records are independently verified here.
