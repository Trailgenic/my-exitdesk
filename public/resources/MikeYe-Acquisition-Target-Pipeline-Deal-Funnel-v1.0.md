# Acquisition Target Pipeline & Deal Funnel

**Mike Ye — Practical M&A Operating Library**  
Version 1.0 · September 10, 2026

Use this working template to manage acquisition targets without turning the process into a generic CRM. The goal is to preserve the mandate, distinguish strategic fit from transactability, make the next decision explicit, and retain the institutional memory behind every pass.

## The decision this tool answers

**Where should the Corp Dev team spend its next increment of attention, and what must happen before each target advances?**

A high-priority target is not automatically a good deal. A scarce asset can still be unavailable, unaffordable, untransferable, or poorly supported by evidence. The pipeline should surface those differences before momentum becomes approval.

## Core pipeline fields

| Field | What it is for |
|---|---|
| Target ID | Stable identifier that survives name changes and stage changes. |
| Target / Company | Company or project name. |
| Sector / Geography | Basic operating context. |
| Owner | Person responsible for moving the target to its next decision. |
| Tier | Work-priority tier, not a probability of closing. |
| Stage | Identified, Screened, Contacted, Conversation, NDA, Materials, First Look, LOI, Diligence, Signing, or Closed. |
| Status | Active, Hold, Passed, or Closed. Do not delete passed targets. |
| Strategic Fit | 1–5 assessment against the acquisition mandate. |
| Scarcity | 1–5 assessment of how difficult the capability, access, rights, or position would be to replicate. |
| Seller Willingness | 1–5 view of whether the owners appear willing to transact. |
| Control Clarity | 1–5 view of whether the identified owners can credibly deliver the required control. |
| Evidence Quality | Unverified, Partial, or Verified. This describes the evidence, not whether the answer is favorable. |
| Estimated EV / Private Price Ceiling | Keep the market estimate separate from the buyer’s private hard ceiling. |
| Last Touch / Action Due | Make stale work and missed commitments visible. |
| Next Decision | The actual gate in front of the team. |
| Next Action / Action Owner | One concrete action and one accountable owner. |
| Key Dependency / Risk | The issue most likely to change willingness to proceed. |
| Consequence | Investigate, Price, Protect, or Walk. |
| Pass Reason / Reopen Trigger | Preserve why the team said no and what would need to change. |
| Source / Evidence | Link or reference supporting the current assessment. |

## Priority Score

The Excel workbook uses a bounded 0–100 work-ordering score. It is not a valuation, expected return, or close probability.

- Strategic Fit: up to 35 points
- Scarcity: up to 25 points
- Seller Willingness: up to 15 points
- Control Clarity: up to 10 points
- Evidence Quality: up to 10 points
- Tier: up to 5 points
- Stale and overdue items reduce the score

A fatal control, integrity, financing, transferability, or price-ceiling issue overrides the score.

## Stage map

| Stage | Minimum question before advance |
|---|---|
| Identified | Why does this target belong in the mandate? |
| Screened | Does it pass the required strategic and transactability criteria? |
| Contacted | Is there a credible route to the owner or decision maker? |
| Conversation | What is the seller posture and control path? |
| NDA | Can the buyer obtain the information needed without compromising required rights or process constraints? |
| Materials | Are the financial, ownership, customer, and dependency materials sufficient for a first look? |
| First Look | Does standalone value, downside, and the private ceiling support further commitment? |
| LOI | Are core economics, funding, major conditions, and exclusivity logic acceptable? |
| Diligence | Does evidence support, weaken, or reopen the thesis? |
| Signing | Are final economics, financing, conditions, and Day 1 readiness acceptable? |
| Closed | Has the deal been handed from transaction execution to value delivery? |

## The Pass Log

A pass is not wasted work. Record:

- Target and stage at pass
- Pass date
- Reason category
- Detailed rationale
- Reopen trigger
- Signal worth monitoring
- Owner and review date
- Evidence/source
- Whether the target was later reopened

The test is simple: six months later, the team should be able to answer **why we passed, what would need to change, and what signal would make us look again.**

## The Evidence Register

Use one row per material claim or unresolved question. Capture the claim, evidence type, source, source date, review date, evidence quality, whether it supports or disconfirms the current thesis, decision impact, consequence, owner, and next evidence needed.

Unknowns are findings. They should create a specific next action instead of an assumed favorable answer.

## The Decision Log

For every consequential commitment, preserve:

- Decision date
- Target and gate
- Decision: Advance, Hold, Rework, Pass, Submit LOI, Enter Exclusivity, Sign, or Close
- Decision owner
- Rationale and evidence references
- Estimated value and private ceiling at the time
- Investigate / Price / Protect / Walk consequence
- Conditions
- Reopen trigger
- Next decision date

The point is not bureaucracy. It is to stop the team from rewriting what it believed after the facts change.

## Mike’s governing judgment

**Frame before detail.** The target must serve the mandate; do not rewrite the mandate merely to accommodate a target the team likes.

**Separate strategic fit from transactability.** A wonderful asset may still be a bad target today if the owners cannot or will not sell, control cannot transfer, or required rights cannot be obtained.

**Keep the ceiling private.** Competitive tension and strategic excitement do not make the business more valuable or the downside more survivable.

**Pass without erasing the work.** The best pipeline is not the one with the most targets. It is the one that makes good use of attention and gets smarter every time the team advances or passes.
